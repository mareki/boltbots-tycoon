# Competitive Benchmarking & Differentiation

**Research snapshot:** 24 October 2026. Player counts and visits change continuously. Sources are descriptions and market research, not internal revenue or retention dashboards; direct in-game UX/shop studies still must be performed.

## 1. What does "the most successful" mean?

There is no single verified "most profitable factory tycoon" in public data: concurrent players, cumulative visits, revenue, retention and creator profit are different measures. Rankings mix loosely defined tycoon/simulator tags and can be dominated by genres that are not truly factory automation.

Across Roblox generally, games like **Grow a Garden** and **Steal a Brainrot** have demonstrated massive visit scale, while direct factory-automation comparisons are smaller and structurally different. Newzoo documents the 2025 concentration and fast rise/fall of hit experiences. We therefore choose **multiple function-specific exemplars** rather than clone one popular title.

## 2. Benchmark matrix (observed vs. proposed transfer)

| Product | Publicly observable features / credible description | Transferable design principle | Distinctive RFT interpretation | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Industrialist** | Production lines, upgrades, research, pollution; descriptions warn about steep learning curve | Systemic satisfaction from visible factories | Compact modular robot line with few predictable early decisions | Deep complexity / visual identity / machine model replication |
| **Grow a Garden** | Seeds -> wait -> harvest -> profit; world also grows while offline; display garden to friends | Easy-to-grasp loop, low barrier, visible return progress | Robot workshop builds; capped optional passive output only after validation | Near-identical loop, plant themes, pressure to log in |
| **Pet Simulator 99** | Collect pets, coins, open eggs, explore, events/clans | Collection goals and clear catalog visibility | Unique functional robot blueprints with deterministic unlocks | Copying pets/rarity UI, paid/random egg mechanics |
| **Car Dealership Tycoon** | Build dealership, vehicles, customization, races, challenges, special events | Personal identity through a collection; update cadence | Display robot fleets and themed factory looks | Vehicle artwork, map, marketing/season branding |
| **Build Your Factory Tycoon** | Factory/conveyor building in Roblox experience listing | Genre expectations: readable machinery, visible goods movement | Assembly as visually expressive robot creation | Exact world layout or models |

Public sources and URLs are in `SOURCES.md`. The transfers in this table are hypotheses requiring direct gameplay inspection, not proof that a mechanic causes revenue/retention.

## 3. Feature priority framework

Evaluate each possible "borrowed" game design idea on:

1. **Player value:** clear, enjoyable, understandable?
2. **Differentiation:** translated into robots and factory expression rather than recognizable copying?
3. **MVP cost:** can 1-2 agents implement a complete vertical slice?
4. **Business fit:** encourages naturally recurring use without payment pressure?
5. **Fairness and platform compliance:** especially for younger users?
6. **Evidence:** can we inspect behavior or is it speculation based on charts?

Initial priorities: visible assembly **high**, first-minute progress **high**, robot collection **high**, cosmetic expression **high**, co-op factory **later**, unlimited offline rewards **no**, paid random eggs **no**, stealing/raiding **no**.

## 4. Required hands-on competitive study (B1 task)

A browsing/text-only comparison cannot substitute for 30–45 minutes of observing actual onboarding. When owner authorizes Roblox gameplay exploration, the research agent (or user-assisted Studio/browser workflow) should complete for **Industrialist, Grow a Garden, Pet Simulator 99, Car Dealership Tycoon**:

- Timed timeline: first 0–60s, 1–5m, 5–15m; record each instruction, goal, reward, CTA and user decision.
- Screenshots/video only for **internal commentary and analysis** (not redistribution or tracing of protected art).
- Shop audit: item names, displayed Robux costs, repeatability, prompts, timing and disclosures (at actual observation date).
- Interaction audit: desktop vs touch, session blockers, accessibility.
- Mechanics chart: what specifically creates a visual payoff or new goal?
- Evidence grade per finding: `OBSERVED`, `LISTING-ONLY`, `INFERRED`, `UNKNOWN`.
- Write at least five strong insights and five anti-patterns with citations and images labeled by source.

Document results in `docs/COMPETITOR_OBSERVATION_LOG.md`; do not make product changes from `INFERRED` findings without a hypothesis + evaluation plan.

## 5. RFT's defensible "best-of" formulation

- Take **Industrialist's** satisfaction of automated movement, but remove its intimidating tutorial burden for the first 10 minutes.
- Take **Grow a Garden's** simple readable action->reward loop, but make output visually transform robot models rather than wait on plants.
- Take **Pet Simulator 99's** collection clarity, but use deterministic blueprint achievements and unique robots rather than randomized monetized eggs.
- Take **Car Dealership Tycoon's** expression and shareable display, but showcase a personal assembly hall and robot lineup.

**Not a clone:** our machine designs, silhouettes, icons, UI structure, brand name, sound, animations, progression tuning and code must be independently authored.

## 6. Business reality check

Competitor public Visits/CCU tell us about visibility, not net revenue or profitability. Newzoo's fast top-chart swings are a warning against building a financial model from peak visits alone. Forecast RFT using **its own** retention, spend and player acquisition data after a private/public test.
